# Writing rules for user-facing output

The rubric's End-to-End Quality line (20 pts) requires output with "the finish of something a person would sign their name to rather than an obvious AI generated draft." These rules bind the product's user-facing output AND the judge-facing docs (README, REPRODUCE, HOT_TAKE). Not code, not commits.

## Banned words

utilize (use) · leverage as a verb (use) · delve (dig into) · comprehensive (complete) · robust (solid) · seamless (smooth) · facilitate (help) · furthermore / additionally / moreover (and, also, or cut) · "it is important to note" (state the fact) · cutting-edge · "plays a crucial role" (matters).

## Banned patterns

- "It's not just X, it's Y" and every "more than just a" construction
- Rule of three as a default rhythm — vary list lengths
- Self-narration: "This highlights / underscores / demonstrates" — show the evidence instead
- Throat-clearing openers: "To understand X, we first need to…"
- Unearned punchy one-liners: "The results speak for themselves."
- Fake-balanced hedging: "While X has its merits, it's not without challenges."
- Empty transitions and summary sentences that restate the section
- Em-dash as a rhythmic emphasis tic
- Over-qualification stacks ("generally", "typically", "arguably") until nothing commits

## The two tells to self-check

1. Explaining significance instead of showing it — cut sentences that tell.
2. Symmetry — equal bullet lengths, parallel clauses, uniform section depth. Real writing is lumpy: some points earn paragraphs, others four words.
